Overview

Player Retention

Player retention refers to a game's ability to keep its user base engaged over time. In the competitive gaming market, understanding why players stay or leave is critical for studios and publishers looking to maximize lifetime value and community growth.

Our approach to player retention integrates supervised learning, error analysis, and a similarity-based recommendation system to predict churn, understand its underlying drivers, and identify competing games. We will be able to observe actionable insights into player engagement, personalization, and retention strategies in gaming ecosystems.

We analyze player behavior patterns, engagement distributions, and churn indicators using visualization, statistical summaries, and feature-level exploration across multiple dimensions of gameplay.

More Info

Feature Distributions

We analyze the distribution of numerical features such as playtime, session frequency, and review counts using histograms to understand player behavior patterns and skewness.

Outlier Detection

Box plots reveal extreme values in engagement metrics, helping identify anomalies such as unusually high playtime or irregular user activity that may distort model predictions.

Data Sampling Strategy

We apply random sampling (50,000 rows) to ensure computational feasibility while preserving statistically representative insights across the full dataset.

Automated Profiling

Using YData Profiling, we generate comprehensive reports covering feature distributions, correlations, missing values, and data quality to accelerate exploratory analysis.

Correlation Analysis

Heatmaps and pairplots expose relationships between features, highlighting which variables co-vary with churn risk and informing feature selection for downstream models.

Engagement Segmentation

Players are grouped into activity tiers based on session frequency and playtime, enabling targeted analysis of behavioral differences across cohorts.

Review Sentiment

Text analysis on user reviews surfaces sentiment signals that correlate with player satisfaction, providing qualitative depth to complement behavioral data.

Temporal Patterns

Time-series decomposition reveals daily and weekly rhythms in player activity, helping identify peak engagement windows and early warning signs of disengagement.

Challenges

Steam data is sparse and noisy — many players have minimal interaction histories, making it difficult to build reliable behavioral profiles from limited signals.

Class imbalance between churned and retained players requires careful handling through oversampling, weighted loss functions, and threshold tuning to avoid biased predictions.

Solutions

We apply feature imputation, temporal aggregation, and cold-start strategies to extract meaningful signals even from sparse user profiles.

Ensemble methods with calibrated probabilities and SMOTE-based resampling address class imbalance while preserving model generalizability across diverse player segments.
