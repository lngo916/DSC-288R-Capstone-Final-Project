Overview

Player Retention

Player retention refers to a game's ability to keep its user base engaged over time. In the competitive gaming market, understanding why players stay or leave is critical for studios and publishers looking to maximize lifetime value and community growth.

Our approach to player retention integrates supervised learning, error analysis, and a similarity-based recommendation system to predict churn, understand its underlying drivers, and identify competing games. We will be able to observe actionable insights into player engagement, personalization, and retention strategies in gaming ecosystems.

Machine Learning

Model Exploration

A PySpark ML pipeline trained on Steam behavioral data across five classifiers — from linear baselines to gradient-boosted trees — to identify players at risk of churning and surface retention strategies.

01   Pipeline overview
01
Data ingestion
Train, val, and test splits loaded from Parquet via PySpark. Full data runs on Expanse HPC; sampled data runs on Colab. Columnar storage enables efficient feature assembly.
02
Feature engineering
10 behavioral columns assembled into a dense feature vector via VectorAssembler — playtime, review activity, vote signals, and early-access flag.
03
Churn labeling
Binary label derived from author_playtime_last_two_weeks: ≤ 60 min → churned (1), else retained (0).
04
Model training
Five classifiers trained independently: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, Random Forest, SVM, and XGBoost — each timed and evaluated on the validation split.
05
Evaluation
Models scored on AUC-ROC, AUC-PR, F1, weighted precision/recall, and accuracy for both train and val splits to surface generalization gaps.
06
Strategic output
Churn probabilities translated into retention actions — personalized notifications and game recommendations for high-risk players.
Churn definition Players with ≤ 60 min playtime in the past two weeks are labeled as churned.
churn  = 1   if  author_playtime_last_two_weeks  ≤ 60 min
churn  = 0   otherwise

Model Comparison

We evaluated multiple modeling approaches to predict Steam player churn, ranging from interpretable baselines to deep sequential models that capture long-term engagement patterns.

XGBoost

Primary production model for churn prediction. Captures non-linear interactions between behavioral features such as playtime, review sentiment, and engagement frequency. Strong performance on structured tabular Steam data.

Random Forest

Baseline ensemble model used for robustness comparison and feature importance validation. Helps verify stability of churn signals across different sampling and feature subsets.

Logistic Regression

Interpretable baseline model used to establish linear separability of churn behavior. Provides a transparent benchmark for understanding directional impact of key features like early playtime and review activity.

LSTM / Sequential Model

Captures temporal dynamics in player behavior over time (session sequences, play history). Used to model long-term engagement trends that static models cannot represent.

Challenges

Steam data is sparse and noisy — many players have minimal interaction histories, making it difficult to build reliable behavioral profiles from limited signals.

Class imbalance between churned and retained players requires careful handling through oversampling, weighted loss functions, and threshold tuning to avoid biased predictions.

Solutions

We apply feature imputation, temporal aggregation, and cold-start strategies to extract meaningful signals even from sparse user profiles.

Ensemble methods with calibrated probabilities and SMOTE-based resampling address class imbalance while preserving model generalizability across diverse player segments.
