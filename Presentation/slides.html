Project Overview
Predicting Player Churn on Steam at Scale
Our project investigates how behavioral gameplay patterns, review sentiment, and engagement frequency can be used to predict player churn on Steam. Using large-scale interaction data and machine learning models including Logistic Regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost, we analyze retention drivers, engineer predictive behavioral features, and evaluate model performance through precision, recall, and AUC-ROC metrics.
70%
Most Steam players disengage within two weeks of installing a game. Detection of early churn allows developers to improve retention strategies, personalize engagement, and reduce long-term player loss before temporary inactivity turns permanent.
Motivation
113M+
Our combined dataset contains over 113 million Steam reviews from 37 million unique players across 106 thousand games. It captures significant features such as gameplay duration, review sentiment, engagement activity, voting behavior, and temporal interaction patterns at a massive scale.
Data Scale
4
We built predictive features grounded in EDA findings and churn literature, focusing on behavioral intensity, recency, and engagement dynamics. We then paired this with Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, and LSTM models to classify retained vs. churned players.
Models
0.91
Our top model achieves 91% AUC-ROC with 87% precision and 83% recall, identifying at-risk players before they disengage.

Literature Survey

Key research informing our approach to player churn prediction, behavioral feature engineering, and recommendation systems on Steam.

01
Churn
Prediction

Runge et al. (2014) demonstrated that early session patterns predict mobile game churn with >80% accuracy using logistic regression on first-week behavioral logs.

Behavioral Logs
02
Gradient
Boosting

Chen & Guestrin (2016) introduced XGBoost, showing superior performance on sparse interaction matrices — directly motivating our primary churn classification model.

XGBoost
03
Sequential
Modeling

Hochreiter & Schmidhuber (1997) established LSTMs for sequential data. Applied to session logs, LSTMs capture temporal decay in player engagement windows.

LSTM
04
Collaborative
Filtering

Koren et al. (2009) proposed SVD++ for matrix factorization on sparse user-item interactions, forming the basis of our Steam game recommendation system.

SVD++
05
Steam
Analytics

Drachen et al. (2016) analyzed Steam telemetry data showing playtime, session frequency, and social features as the strongest predictors of long-term player retention.

Exploratory Data Analysis
Findings
Univariate
Playtime Distributions
Playtime-related features show strong right skew. Most players showed low activity levels, while a small subset accumulated high playtime values.
Engagement
Sparse Engagement Signals
Engagement metrics such as votes, comments, and funny reactions were heavily sparse. Most observations contained low values with few dominating highly engaged users, which suggests uneven participation across the player base.
Categorical
Class Imbalances
Categorical variables such as voted_up and written_during_early_access exhibited class imbalances, indicating that certain player behaviors and review patterns occured more frequently than others. We will consider this aspect for downstream modeling and retention prediction.
Correlation
Behavioral Correlations
We have identified strong positive correlations between playtime and engagement metrics. Users with higher playtime tended to leave more reviews and receive more interaction, suggesting interconnected behavioral engagement patterns.
Recency
Recency & Churn
Inactivity (recency) exhibited a negative relationship with engagement such that players inactive for longer periods were more likely to churn. As so, recency emerged as a critical predictor of player retention.
Outliers
Outlier Detection
Boxplot analysis revealed significant outliers in playtime and engagement features. A small amount of users exhibited disproportionately high activity levels, highlighting the existence of extreme behavioral cases within the dataset.
Profiling
Automated Profiling Reports
We utilized YData Profiling and SweetViz to automate feature exploration such that reports summarsized distributions, missing values, correlations, and data quality metrics.
Retention
Behavior Insights
Behavioral engagement metrics collectively contributed to retention outcomes. Highly active or engaged players consistently demonstrated lower churn likelihood, suggesting that retention is strongly tied to sustained platform interaction.
